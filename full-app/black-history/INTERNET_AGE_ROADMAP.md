# YEP Modern Black American Economic History | Curriculum map
**Scope:** FULL APP, not POC. Historical core + Internet Age + current developments. Editorially source-checked and age-adapted before youth publication.

## Eight connected time periods
1. **Foundations (Before 1865):** Skilled trades, apprenticeships, farming, maritime trade, early Black enterprises, ownership constraints
2. **Freedom and Ownership (1865–1900):** Reconstruction, land, banking, schools, cooperative institutions, exclusion and legal rights
3. **Industrial Enterprise (1900–1945):** Manufacturing, patents, transportation, construction, business districts, financial institutions
4. **Infrastructure and Civil Rights (1945–1975):** Contracts, labor, engineering, desegregation, local institution building, access to capital
5. **Personal Computing (1975–1995):** Computer hardware, engineering teams, patents, business systems, early digital networks
6. **Internet and Enterprise Tech (1990–2010):** Networks, ISPs, broadband, enterprise service contracting, software, security, websites and e-commerce
7. **Mobile and Platform Economy (2005–2020):** Smartphones, apps, digital payments, small-business platforms, online reputation and logistics
8. **AI and Next-Generation Ownership (2020–present):** AI infrastructure, data centers, automation, cloud, cybersecurity, digital rights and emerging businesses

## What the curriculum must cover across all eras
- How people learned work and built trades, inventions and institutions
- The actual customer, product or public need; business or invention operating model
- Ownership structures, funding, financing, distribution, supply chain, hiring and risk
- Historical laws and structures that restricted opportunity and the practical responses to those barriers
- Recorded Black men's contributions and entrepreneurship through a male-centered youth teaching lens, while remaining historically accurate about all other contributors
- Difference between inventions, companies, patents, purchases, CEOs, investors, employees and contractors
- Digital systems and business models: personal computing, networks, broadband, e-commerce, cybersecurity, software-as-a-service, digital advertising, digital payments, cloud, AI and responsible data use
- Cincinnati/Ohio contemporary businesses and builders only after verifying people and records, and with consent for new interviews

## Non-negotiable history rules
1. Do not say one person "invented the Internet" when the technology was developed through many people, institutions and decades. Name specific patented, implemented, documented contributions.
2. Do not claim a founder was "first" without research-grade primary evidence and careful definition of first.
3. Separate the company or subject's own biography from an independent source when possible.
4. Describe each claim's date, historical context, original record, and verification status.
5. A tech founder's success is not evidence that a student can replicate the same result; explain conditions, dependencies, timing, failure and risk.
6. No automatic AI publication of unverified historical claims, quotes, portraits or invented archival artifacts.
7. Youth may compare historical and modern markets, but should not be steered toward risky investment or told guaranteed returns are possible.

## Lesson object and learning loop
- Observe a historical or modern scene (visual)
- Listen to a short age-fitting explanation (captions available)
- Investigate a source or original record
- Make a business or engineering decision
- Receive accurate why/why-not feedback
- Estimate a cost, asset, customer, revenue model or improvement
- Reflect via F.L.A.G.; show evidence for FINISHER facilitator review
- Store dated version and learning record once the full-app learner infrastructure is available
